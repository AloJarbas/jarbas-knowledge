# Proof-study loop: retrieval, worked examples, and interleaving

If proof study is going badly, the problem is often not ambition alone.
It is review design.

A decent proof journal helps, but it still leaves three failure modes wide open:

- you reread instead of retrieving,
- you look at solved examples passively,
- you practice one proof type in a block and mistake fluency for judgment.

## The useful trio

### 1. Retrieval has to be delayed

Do not only retry a proof immediately after writing it.

A better test is:

- once the next day,
- once after a few days,
- once at the weekly review.

If the proof falls apart under delay, then it was not really learned.

## 2. Worked examples should be audited, not admired

A worked example is useful when it answers questions like:

- why this proof method and not another one?
- which definition did the real work?
- which hypothesis was actually load-bearing?
- where would the argument break if the statement changed?

That turns an example into a reusable move instead of a page you recognized once.

## 3. Interleaving trains recognition

Blocked practice makes you good at repeating the current template.
Interleaving makes you decide what kind of problem is in front of you.

For proof practice, that means mixing things like:

- direct proof,
- contradiction,
- contrapositive,
- counterexample,
- induction,
- definition unpacking.

That is closer to what real mathematical work feels like.

## Practical weekly use

A weekly proof review should include all three:

- 2-3 delayed rewrites from memory,
- 1 worked-example audit,
- 1 mixed proof block.

Then classify the week honestly:

- green: keep secondary streams active,
- yellow: keep proof primary and reduce side load,
- red: narrow immediately.
